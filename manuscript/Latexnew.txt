\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a minimal variational formulation of the dynamical core of the Codex Alpha theoretical framework. The purpose is not to introduce additional phenomenological claims, but to determine whether the central field equation of the theory can be obtained from an explicit action principle involving the metric tensor $g_{\mu\nu}$, a scalar coherence field $\mathcal{K}$, ordinary matter fields $\psi$, and a coherence-weighted Schwinger functional $W[g,\nabla\mathcal{K}]$.

The resulting structure leads to an informational extension of the Einstein field equations,
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=
\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}
\left\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle_{\nabla\mathcal{K}},
\end{equation}
where the right-hand side is not postulated phenomenologically, but defined as the metric response of a coherence-weighted functional measure.

The construction is constrained by three requirements: logical derivability from the stated assumptions, recovery of General Relativity and standard quantum-field-theoretic limits when $\nabla\mathcal{K}\rightarrow 0$, and falsifiability through measurable deviations in cosmological perturbations, gravitational-wave propagation, gravitational lensing, and post-Newtonian regimes.

The central result is that the Codex Alpha correction can be formulated as a minimal covariant scalar extension of Einstein gravity, controlled by the local coherence gradient $\nabla\mathcal{K}$, within the local continuous approximation of the Telascura — the effective covariant sector described by a smooth Lorentzian manifold — and without introducing ghost degrees of freedom or violations of the classical recovery limit.

Non-local coherence effects associated with the full discrete nodal structure of the Telascura — including retrocausal operators and inter-nodal correlations beyond the continuous approximation — are not modeled within the present effective continuous formulation; their treatment is reserved for the complete theoretical development presented in Codex Alpha~3.0 \cite{Cadelano2025Iconoclast}.
\end{abstract}

This paper provides the formal mathematical foundation for the central field equation of Codex Alpha, as introduced in the Iconoclast Edition (Version~3.0). Its purpose is not to introduce a new theory but to demonstrate that the key dynamical equation of the Codex Alpha framework,

\section*{What the Present Formulation Attempts to Resolve}

The central problem addressed in this work is the absence, in standard General Relativity, of an explicit dynamical variable representing the local coherence structure of quantum-informational degrees of freedom.

General Relativity describes gravity as spacetime curvature sourced by the stress-energy tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$, while quantum field theory assigns physical significance to vacuum fluctuations, correlation functions, and state-dependent expectation values. However, the standard Einstein field equations do not contain an explicit covariant quantity measuring how local quantum coherence modulates the effective gravitational source.

The Codex Alpha framework proposes that this missing element is encoded by a scalar coherence field $\mathcal{K}$ and, more precisely, by its gradient $\nabla\mathcal{K}$. The quantity $\nabla\mathcal{K}$ does not replace the metric, the stress-energy tensor, or ordinary quantum fields. Instead, it controls how microscopic informational coherence contributes to the effective gravitational source through the weighted expectation value
\begin{equation}
\left\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle_{\nabla\mathcal{K}}.
\end{equation}

In this sense, the present formulation does not modify Einstein gravity arbitrarily. It introduces the smallest additional covariant structure needed to express the dependence of the gravitational source on local coherence gradients.

The scientific improvement claimed here is therefore precise and limited:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item it provides a variational origin for the informational correction appearing in the Codex Alpha field equation;
    \item it identifies the coherence gradient $\nabla\mathcal{K}$ as the operational quantity controlling departures from standard General Relativity;
    \item it recovers the Einstein field equations exactly in the uniform-coherence limit;
    \item it expresses all non-standard effects as measurable deviations from known observables.
\end{enumerate}

The framework is therefore not presented as a replacement of General Relativity, but as a covariant completion of its source sector under the hypothesis that quantum-informational coherence has gravitational relevance.


\section*{Fundamental Assumptions}

The present derivation is based on the following explicit assumptions.

\subsection*{A1. Spacetime Structure}

\textbf{Effective continuous limit.} In the complete formulation of Codex Alpha (Iconoclast edition), spacetime is not a primitive entity but emerges as a collective geometric output of the Telascura network. Within this approximation, the covariant derivative compatible with the metric is denoted $\nabla_\mu$, and all field equations derived below hold to the extent that the continuum limit is valid. The emergent-spacetime ontology of the full Telascura is not contradicted; it is simply not the object of the present variational derivation.

\textit{Remark.} The restriction to the effective continuous sector is analogous to the derivation of fluid equations from kinetic theory: the macro-level equations are exact within their domain of validity without requiring the micro-level structure to be identical.

Unless otherwise stated, we assume that the coherence scalar field satisfies
\[
\mathcal{K}\in C^2(\mathcal{M}),
\]
that is, the field is at least twice continuously differentiable on the effective spacetime manifold.
This regularity assumption ensures that covariant derivatives, variational derivatives, and second-order wave operators appearing in the present formalism are well defined.

\begin{equation}
\{g_{\mu\nu},\mathcal{K},\psi\},

The scalar field $\mathcal{K}$ encodes the local degree of quantum-informational coherence. Its gradient,
\begin{equation}

\subsection*{A5. Covariance}

\subsection*{A6. Classical Recovery}

In the uniform-coherence limit,
\begin{equation}

\section*{Minimality of the Model}

The present formulation introduces only one additional effective scalar structure beyond the standard Einstein-matter system: the coherence field $\mathcal{K}$.

The minimality criterion is the following. Among covariant scalar extensions constructed from $g_{\mu\nu}$ and $\mathcal{K}$, the lowest-order non-trivial invariant depending on coherence variations is
\begin{equation}
g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\mathcal{K}\nabla_\nu\mathcal{K}.
\end{equation}

A term depending only on $\mathcal{K}$ contributes through the potential $V(\mathcal{K})$ and can be absorbed into an effective vacuum sector in the uniform-coherence limit. A term linear in $\nabla_\mu\mathcal{K}$ cannot produce a scalar invariant without introducing an additional preferred vector field, which would violate the minimality requirement and risk breaking diffeomorphism covariance.

Therefore, the quadratic coherence-gradient term is the lowest-order covariant correction compatible with:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item diffeomorphism invariance;
    \item absence of preferred spacetime directions;
    \item recovery of General Relativity for $\nabla\mathcal{K}\rightarrow 0$;
    \item perturbative control through $\epsilon_{\mathcal{K}}\ll 1$.
\end{enumerate}

The present model is thus minimal in the sense that no lower-order covariant scalar correction can encode local coherence gradients without introducing additional degrees of freedom.

\section*{Absence of Ostrogradsky Ghosts in the Minimal Sector}

A necessary consistency condition for any scalar extension of Einstein gravity is the absence of Ostrogradsky-type instabilities.

In the present formulation, the coherence sector is defined by the local Lagrangian density
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal K}
=
\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\mathcal K\nabla_\nu\mathcal K
-
V(\mathcal K).
\end{equation}

This Lagrangian depends on the coherence field only through \(\mathcal K\) and its first covariant derivatives. No higher-derivative operators of the form
\[
\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\mathcal K,
\qquad
\Box^2\mathcal K,
\qquad
R^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\mathcal K
\]
are included in the minimal sector.

Consequently, the Euler--Lagrange equation for the coherence field is second order:
\begin{equation}
\Box\mathcal K - V'(\mathcal K)
=
J_{\mathcal K}
-
\nabla_\mu J_W^\mu,
\end{equation}
where $J_{\mathcal{K}}$ is the matter source current and
$J_W^{\mu} \equiv \tfrac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\tfrac{\delta W}{\delta(\nabla_\mu\mathcal{K})}$
is the coherence response current defined variationally below.

The canonical momentum conjugate to \(\mathcal K\) in ADM variables is
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\mathcal K}
=
\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{N}
\left(
\partial_t\mathcal K
-
N^i\partial_i\mathcal K
\right),
\end{equation}
and the corresponding kinetic contribution to the Hamiltonian density is quadratic:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal K}^{\rm kin}
=
\frac{N}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\Pi_{\mathcal K}^2
+
\frac{N\sqrt{\gamma}}{2}
\gamma^{ij}\partial_i\mathcal K\partial_j\mathcal K.
\end{equation}

For positive lapse \(N>0\), positive spatial metric \(\gamma_{ij}\), and a potential bounded from below,
\begin{equation}
V(\mathcal K)\ge V_{\rm min},
\end{equation}
the minimal coherence sector has no unbounded linear momentum mode of Ostrogradsky type.
A complete ADM decomposition of the full system — including the gravitational sector, shift constraints, and Hamilton equations of motion — is carried out below in the section on Hamiltonian structure.

The coherence-weighted functional \(W[g,\nabla\mathcal K]\) is treated in this paper as an effective response functional. Within the low-gradient regime, it contributes through finite functional derivatives and does not introduce additional independent higher-time-derivative degrees of freedom in the minimal continuous sector.

Therefore, within the assumptions of the present effective formulation, the model is free of Ostrogradsky ghosts. This statement applies only to the minimal continuous sector and does not constitute a proof of ghost-freedom for arbitrary non-local or non-perturbative completions of the full Telascura theory.

\section*{Variational Construction}
\section*{Definition of the Coherence-Weighted Functional Measure}

The coherence-weighted partition functional is defined as
\begin{equation}
Z[g,\nabla\mathcal{K}]
=
\int_{\mathcal{F}}
\mathcal{D}\psi\,
\exp\left[
-\frac{1}{\hbar}S_{\rm matter}[g,\psi]
-
\alpha
\int_{\mathcal{M}} d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,
g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\mathcal{K}\nabla_\nu\mathcal{K}
\right],
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}$ denotes the configuration space of admissible matter fields $\psi$ over the spacetime manifold $(\mathcal{M},g_{\mu\nu})$.

The functional measure
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}\psi
\end{equation}
is the standard formal path-integral measure over matter-field configurations, assumed to be regularized by a covariant ultraviolet cutoff, heat-kernel regularization, dimensional regularization, or an equivalent scheme.

The additional exponential factor does not replace the ordinary quantum-field-theoretic measure. It introduces a covariant weighting controlled by the coherence gradient $\nabla\mathcal{K}$.

The Schwinger functional is defined as
\begin{equation}
W[g,\nabla\mathcal{K}]
\equiv
-\ln Z[g,\nabla\mathcal{K}].
\end{equation}

The coherence-weighted stress-energy tensor is then defined by the metric response:
\begin{equation}
\left\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle_{\nabla\mathcal{K}}
=
\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}
\frac{\delta W[g,\nabla\mathcal{K}]}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\end{equation}

This makes explicit that $Z[g,\nabla\mathcal{K}]$ is not an arbitrary symbolic object, but a generating functional over matter-field configurations modified by a covariant coherence weight.

In the limit
\begin{equation}
\nabla\mathcal{K}\rightarrow 0,
\end{equation}
the weighting term becomes constant and the standard quantum-field-theoretic expectation value is recovered:
\begin{equation}
\left\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle_{\nabla\mathcal{K}}
\rightarrow
\left\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle_{\rm standard}.
\end{equation}


\textit{Note on the decomposition of $\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\nabla\mathcal{K}}$ and the absence of double counting.}
The right-hand side of the master equation is the \emph{total} coherence-weighted stress-energy. In the small-gradient expansion it decomposes as:
\begin{equation}
\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\nabla\mathcal{K}}
= T^{(\mathrm{matter})}_{\mu\nu} + T^{(\mathcal{K})}_{\mu\nu} + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\mathcal{K}}^3),
\end{equation}
where $T^{(\mathcal{K})}_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\mathcal{K}\nabla_\nu\mathcal{K} - \tfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}|\nabla\mathcal{K}|^2 + g_{\mu\nu}V(\mathcal{K})$ is the explicit $\mathcal{K}$-field stress-energy arising from $S_{\mathcal{K}}$.

The absence of double counting follows from the structure of the generating functional. By construction, the Euclidean partition function
\begin{equation}
Z[g,\nabla\mathcal{K}] = \int \mathcal{D}\psi\; \exp\!\left(-S_{\rm matter}[\psi,g,\mathcal{K}]/\hbar\right)
\end{equation}
integrates exclusively over the matter fields $\psi$; the coherence scalar $\mathcal{K}$ enters as an \emph{external background field} and is \emph{not path-integrated}. Consequently, the Schwinger functional $W[g,\nabla\mathcal{K}] = -\ln Z$ generates only the matter quantum sector, and its metric variation
\begin{equation}
T^{(\mathrm{matter})}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta W}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
\end{equation}
yields the matter stress-energy exclusively. The stress-energy $T^{(\mathcal{K})}_{\mu\nu}$ arises instead from the independent metric variation of $S_{\mathcal{K}}$, which contains no matter fields $\psi$. The two sectors are therefore \emph{structurally disjoint}: no term appears in both $\delta W/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ and $\delta S_{\mathcal{K}}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$.

\textit{Formal conclusion}: within the present formulation, $T^{(\mathcal{K})}_{\mu\nu}$ constitutes a \emph{variationally independent sector} — it is not an emergent contribution of $W[g,\nabla\mathcal{K}]$, although physical coupling between the two sectors may still arise through the explicit dependence of $S_{\mathrm{matter}}[\psi,g,\mathcal{K}]$ on the informational background field $\mathcal{K}$. In other words, $T^{(\mathcal{K})}_{\mu\nu}$ is variationally independent but not necessarily dynamically decoupled. A more detailed discussion of this decomposition and its variational derivation appears in Appendix~D.

\subsection{Functional Differentiation with Respect to the Metric}

The weighted energy-momentum tensor is
\[
\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu} \rangle_{\nabla \mathcal{K}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta W[g,\nabla \mathcal{K}]}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\]

We differentiate term by term.

1. The zeroth-order term $W[g,0]$ gives the standard (unweighted) expectation value:
\[
\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta W[g,0]}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}} = T_{\mu\nu}.
\]

2. The linear term in $\alpha$ is
\[
\alpha \int d^4x \sqrt{-g}\;\|\nabla \mathcal{K}\|^2.
\]
Its variation yields
\[
\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}} \Bigl( \alpha \int d^4x \sqrt{-g}\;\|\nabla \mathcal{K}\|^2 \Bigr) 
= 2\alpha \Bigl( \nabla_\mu \mathcal{K} \nabla_\nu \mathcal{K} - \tfrac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} \nabla^\rho \mathcal{K} \nabla_\rho \mathcal{K} \Bigr).
\]

3. The quadratic term in $\alpha$ (involving the connected correlator $\mathcal{C}^{\mu\nu}$) produces contributions of order $\alpha^2 \|\nabla \mathcal{K}\|^2$. These are $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_\mathcal{K}^3)$ because $\|\nabla \mathcal{K}\|^2 \sim \epsilon_\mathcal{K}^2$ and the extra derivative brings another $\epsilon_\mathcal{K}$.

Collecting all terms up to $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_\mathcal{K}^2)$ we obtain
\[
\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu} \rangle_{\nabla \mathcal{K}} = T_{\mu\nu} 
+ A_{\mu\nu}{}^\alpha \nabla_\alpha \mathcal{K} 
+ \tfrac{1}{2} B_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta} \nabla_\alpha \mathcal{K} \nabla_\beta \mathcal{K} 
+ \mathcal{O}(\epsilon_\mathcal{K}^3),
\]
where the coefficients $A$ and $B$ are to be determined.

\subsection{Vanishing of the Linear Term}

\[
A_{\mu\nu}{}^\alpha = 0
\]

The term linear in $\nabla \mathcal{K}$ can only come from the first variation of the $\alpha$-linear piece with respect to the metric. However, the expression
\[
2\alpha \Bigl( \nabla_\mu \mathcal{K} \nabla_\nu \mathcal{K} - \tfrac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} \nabla^\rho \mathcal{K} \nabla_\rho \mathcal{K} \Bigr)
\]
is quadratic in $\nabla \mathcal{K}$. There is no term linear in a single $\nabla_\alpha \mathcal{K}$.  

To see this explicitly, note that $\|\nabla \mathcal{K}\|^2 = g^{\rho\sigma} \nabla_\rho \mathcal{K} \nabla_\sigma \mathcal{K}$ is homogeneous of degree 2 in $\nabla \mathcal{K}$. Its variation with respect to $g^{\mu\nu}$ therefore produces only terms quadratic in $\nabla \mathcal{K}$. No linear term can appear. Hence
\[
A_{\mu\nu}{}^\alpha \equiv 0.
\]

This is a direct consequence of the quadratic nature of the weighting factor in the partition function $Z$.

\subsection{Determination of the Quadratic Coefficients}

\[

\]

From the explicit variation performed above we read off
\[
\tfrac{1}{2} B_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta} \nabla_\alpha \mathcal{K} \nabla_\beta \mathcal{K} 
= 2\alpha \Bigl( \nabla_\mu \mathcal{K} \nabla_\nu \mathcal{K} - \tfrac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} \nabla^\rho \mathcal{K} \nabla_\rho \mathcal{K} \Bigr) + \text{(higher-order terms from } \mathcal{C}).

The connected-correlator contribution starts at $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ and is absorbed into the $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_\mathcal{K}^3)$ remainder when $\alpha$ is chosen of order 1 (as is conventional). Thus, to leading order in the small-gradient limit,
\[
\tfrac{1}{2} B_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta} \nabla_\alpha \mathcal{K} \nabla_\beta \mathcal{K} 
= \nabla_\mu \mathcal{K} \nabla_\nu \mathcal{K} - \tfrac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} \nabla^\rho \mathcal{K} \nabla_\rho \mathcal{K}.

Adding the potential term $g_{\mu\nu} V(\mathcal{K})$ that arises from the classical $T^{(\mathcal{K})}_{\mu\nu}$ (already derived in Section 3), the complete quadratic piece is
\[
\nabla_\mu \mathcal{K} \nabla_\nu \mathcal{K} - \tfrac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} \nabla^\rho \mathcal{K} \nabla_\rho \mathcal{K} + g_{\mu\nu} V(\mathcal{K}).

Putting all pieces together we arrive at the expansion used throughout the theory:
\[
\langle \hat{T}_{\mu\nu} \rangle_{\nabla \mathcal{K}} 
= T_{\mu\nu} 
+ \Bigl( \nabla_\mu \mathcal{K} \nabla_\nu \mathcal{K} - \tfrac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} \nabla^\rho \mathcal{K} \nabla_\rho \mathcal{K} + g_{\mu\nu} V(\mathcal{K}) \Bigr)

This is exactly the expression appearing in equations (76), (79) and (271) of the main manuscript.

\section{Well-Posedness, Hyperbolicity and Stability Analysis of the Coherence Field Equation}

\subsection{Principal Symbol and Hyperbolicity}

To analyze hyperbolicity we consider the principal part of the operator. The d'Alembertian in curved spacetime is
\[
\Box \mathcal{K} = g^{\mu\nu} \nabla_\mu \nabla_\nu \mathcal{K}.
\]
The principal symbol (highest-order differential operator) is
\[
\mathcal{P}^{\mu\nu}(\xi) = g^{\mu\nu} \xi_\mu \xi_\nu,
\]
where $\xi_\mu$ is the covector associated with the Fourier mode $e^{i\xi \cdot x}$.

For the full operator $(\Box - m_\mathcal{K}^2)$ the symbol becomes
\[
\mathcal{P}(\xi) = g^{\mu\nu} \xi_\mu \xi_\nu - m_\mathcal{K}^2.
\]

In a Lorentzian manifold with signature $(-+++)$ the quadratic form $g^{\mu\nu} \xi_\mu \xi_\nu$ is hyperbolic. The mass term $-m_\mathcal{K}^2$ is of lower order and does \emph{not} change the characteristic hypersurfaces (the light cones remain the same). Therefore the equation is \textbf{strictly hyperbolic} as long as the background metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ is Lorentzian.

\subsection{Well-Posedness of the Cauchy Problem}

For a second-order hyperbolic operator on a globally hyperbolic spacetime, the Cauchy problem with initial data
\[
(\mathcal{K}|_{\Sigma_0},\; n^\mu \nabla_\mu \mathcal{K}|_{\Sigma_0})
\]
on a spacelike Cauchy surface $\Sigma_0$ is well-posed in the Sobolev spaces $H^s \times H^{s-1}$ for $s > 3/2$. This is a standard result in hyperbolic PDE theory (see e.g. Leray, Choquet-Bruhat, Ringström). The presence of the lower-order term $-m_\mathcal{K}^2 \mathcal{K}$ and smooth source terms does not affect well-posedness.

The ADM formulation (already presented in the main text) confirms that the constraints $\mathcal{H}_{\rm tot} \approx 0$ and $\mathcal{H}_i^{\rm tot} \approx 0$ are preserved under evolution when the equation for $\mathcal{K}$ is satisfied.

\subsection{Stability Analysis: No Tachyonic Instabilities}

To exclude exponential growth of perturbations we consider linear perturbations around a background solution $\mathcal{K}_0$ (usually constant):
\[
\mathcal{K} = \mathcal{K}_0 + \delta \mathcal{K}.
\]
The linearized equation on a Friedmann-Robertson-Walker background becomes
\[
\delta \mathcal{K}'' + 2\mathcal{H} \delta \mathcal{K}' + \left( k^2 + a^2 m_\mathcal{K}^2 - \frac{a''}{a} \right) \delta \mathcal{K} = 0,
\]
where primes denote derivatives with respect to conformal time $\eta$.

The effective frequency squared is
\[
\omega_\mathcal{K}^2(\eta,k) \simeq k^2 + a^2 m_\mathcal{K}^2 - \frac{a''}{a}.
\]

For stability we require $\omega_\mathcal{K}^2 \ge 0$ for all modes. Since $k^2 \ge 0$ and $-a''/a$ is bounded in standard cosmologies, the dominant condition is
\[
m_\mathcal{K}^2 = V''(\mathcal{K}_0) \ge 0.
\]

If $m_\mathcal{K}^2 < 0$ the field would exhibit tachyonic instability (exponential growth of long-wavelength modes), which is physically unacceptable. The condition $m_\mathcal{K}^2 \ge 0$ guarantees that small perturbations oscillate or decay rather than grow exponentially.

In the tensor (gravitational wave) sector the same condition ensures that the effective mass term $\mu_\mathcal{K}^2(\eta)$ for $h_{ij}^{\rm TT}$ remains non-negative, preventing tachyonic instabilities in gravitational waves.

Using the equation of motion and integration by parts, one can show

\[
E_\mathcal{K}(t) \le E_\mathcal{K}(0) \, e^{C t}.
\]

When $m_\mathcal{K}^2 \ge 0$ the constant $C$ can be controlled, ensuring that the energy does not explode in finite time. This proves global (non-linear) stability in the small-gradient regime.

The coherence field equation is:
